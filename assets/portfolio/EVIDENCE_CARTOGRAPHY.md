# Evidence Cartography

Evidence Cartography treats analytical truth as terrain: precise, layered, and
worthy of patient observation. Space is not filled for effect. It is measured,
surveyed, and opened so that each mark carries the gravity of a coordinate.
Forms behave like instruments rather than decoration—calibrated arcs, quiet
grids, and fields that reveal relationships through position before words.

Color operates as a disciplined signal system. Deep mineral navy holds the
ground; ivory creates breathing room; cyan, acid lime, and restrained coral
mark distinct kinds of evidence. Their interaction must feel meticulously
calibrated, the product of deep expertise rather than a fashionable palette.
Contrast is clinical but never sterile, with every hue earning its place.

Scale builds rhythm through deliberate asymmetry. Monumental numerals sit
beside fine reference marks, turning measurement into architecture. Repetition
suggests trials, samples, and verification without illustrating them
literally. The composition should reward sustained viewing and appear labored
over with painstaking attention, as if every interval had been adjusted across
countless refinements.

Balance comes from tension between confidence and doubt. Strong geometric
anchors state what the evidence supports; interruptions, ruled boundaries, and
open space preserve what it cannot claim. Typography is sparse and integrated
as a visual instrument—short labels, exact values, and one decisive thesis—so
the design communicates through hierarchy rather than explanation.

The finished artifact must feel like an atlas from an imaginary discipline:
an object in which invisible reliability has been mapped with master-level
execution. Craft is visible in alignment, optical spacing, edge quality, and
the restraint to stop. Nothing is ornamental by accident; every detail should
look meticulously crafted by someone at the absolute top of the field.
